Neural Oscillations & Their Discovery

How feedback loops between neurons generate rhythmic frequencies, and how those rhythms were first measured in humans.

FIG 01

How a Rhythm Is Born: The "Go" and "Stop" Loop

Cortical oscillations arise from coupled populations of excitatory neurons (which increase firing) and inhibitory interneurons (which suppress it).

When excitatory cells fire, they activate inhibitory cells that temporarily quiet the circuit. Once inhibition decays, the cycle repeats. The round-trip delay of this feedback loop sets the oscillation frequency in Hertz (Hz).

Loop Speed: 10 Hz

How Humans Discovered Brain Waves (1875 – 1924)

  1. 1875
    Richard Caton (UK): Placed wires directly on animal brains and noticed tiny electrical currents flickered when light was shone in their eyes.
  2. 1924
    Hans Berger (Germany): Placed silver foil strips on his teenage son's scalp—no surgery—and recorded the first human EEG.
  3. KEY
    The Proof: When his son closed his eyes and relaxed, a steady 10 Hz wave appeared. The moment he opened his eyes or did mental math, the wave vanished. That proved the wave came from thinking, not heartbeat or muscle twitch.
