Current Status of BCI Research

A factual summary of capabilities established in clinical practice, active human trials, and unsolved research problems.

FIG 01

Reality Check: Proven vs. Experimental vs. Not Possible

Capability Status Hardware Needed What It Actually Looks Like Today
Detecting Brain States
(Sleep, focus, seizures)		 PROVEN Non-invasive Scalp EEG Used in hospitals every day. Consumer headbands can also track general relaxation vs. focus.
Simple Mental Commands
(Left vs. Right vs. Rest)		 PROVEN Non-invasive Scalp EEG Works reliably enough (~70–90% after per-subject calibration) to drive discrete binary or ternary switches and basic assistive devices.
Cursor & Robotic Arm Control CLINICAL TRIAL Surgical Implant
(Neuralink, Utah Array, Synchron)		 Paralyzed volunteers can move a mouse cursor, play chess, and operate robotic limbs smoothly by imagining hand movement.
Attempted Speech Decoding CLINICAL TRIAL Surgical Implant
(Speech Cortex)		 Patients who lost the ability to speak can try to mouth words; AI decodes the intended speech at ~60–78 words/minute.
Reading Silent Thoughts
(Inner monologue on any topic)		 NOT SOLVED — We cannot decode unconstrained, private inner speech reliably without the person actively trying to speak or train on fixed words.
Memory Transfer / Brain-to-Brain Internet NOT SOLVED — We do not know how memories are stored across billions of synapses well enough to copy, upload, or stream them between people.
FIG 02

Summary of the Five Foundations

  1. 01
    The Brain: Neurons fire tiny electrical pulses; the motor strip on top controls the opposite hand.
  2. 02
    Brain Waves: Feedback loops between "Go" and "Stop" neurons create rhythms like Alpha/Mu (8–12 Hz) and Beta (13–30 Hz).
  3. 03
    What BCI Is: A bridge that reads brain activity directly to bypass muscles.
  4. 04
    How It Works: Brain → Sensor → Clean (8–30 Hz) → Features → AI Model → Action + Feedback.
  5. 05
    Current Scope: Non-invasive EEG reliably supports discrete state and motor-imagery classification; high-bandwidth speech and fine motor control remain limited to surgical trials.
